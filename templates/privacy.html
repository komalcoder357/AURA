Privacy & About

What AURA stores

Your account (email, display name, password hash, optional avatar), conversations and messages, per-message emotion estimates (label, score, method, language), reply timings, memory items you choose to save, and your preferences (theme, voice, language).

What is sent to Gemini

Only your current message, a bounded number of recent messages from the same conversation, and saved memory items relevant to the message. Chat search runs on our own database and is never sent to Gemini.

Voice

Speech recognition and speech output use your browser. In Chrome and Edge, recognised speech may be processed by the browser vendor's online speech service; AURA itself never receives audio, only the final text you submit. Spoken replies use voices installed on your device.

Camera (Webcam demo)

The camera is off until you press Start. Video is shown only on your device and is not recorded, saved or uploaded. AURA contains no expression-classification model, so the demo shows a preview and, where your browser supports it, a face count only.

Deleting data

Deleting a conversation removes its messages and analysis. It does not delete saved memory. Clearing memory removes saved memory items. It does not delete conversations. AURA saves memory only when you explicitly ask (for example “remember that …”) or add it yourself.

What the emotion panel is, and is not

  • The label is a text-based estimate from a classifier (English) or an experimental keyword list (Urdu, Roman Urdu). It is a guess about the text, not about how you feel, and not a diagnosis.
  • “Confidence” is the model's raw score, not a calibrated probability, and not emotional intensity. Intensity is not estimated.
  • “Distress-related signals” is an experimental heuristic over recent messages. It is not a diagnosis, a clinical score or a validated assessment.
  • Facial-expression estimates (if ever added) would be a separate source from text estimates and from what you tell AURA yourself.
  • AURA is an educational assistant, not a doctor, therapist or emergency service. If you are in danger, contact local emergency services or a trusted person.
